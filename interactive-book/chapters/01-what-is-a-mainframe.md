# What is a Mainframe?

A mainframe is a large, powerful computer designed for high-volume, processor-intensive computing. Unlike your laptop or desktop, mainframes are built for **reliability**, **security**, and **massive throughput**.

## Why Mainframes Still Matter

Despite being invented in the 1960s, mainframes process:

- **95%** of all credit card transactions
- **90%** of all airline reservations  
- **80%** of all financial transactions
- Most government and healthcare systems

> "If you've used a credit card today, you've used a mainframe."

## Key Characteristics

### 1. Reliability
Mainframes are designed for **99.999% uptime** (five nines). That's less than 5 minutes of downtime per year. They can hot-swap components, run multiple operating systems simultaneously, and self-heal from many failures.

### 2. Security
Security isn't an add-on—it's built into the hardware. IBM mainframes have dedicated cryptographic processors and the most certified security of any platform.

### 3. Throughput
A single mainframe can process **billions of transactions per day**. Banks don't run on cloud VMs—they run on mainframes.

## The IBM zSeries

Modern IBM mainframes are called **zSeries** (or IBM Z). The "z" stands for "zero downtime." Current models include:

- **z16** (2022) - Built-in AI accelerator
- **z15** (2019) - Enhanced encryption
- **z14** (2017) - Pervasive encryption
